Cookie Policy

Last updated: 20 November 2026
This policy explains cookies and similar browser technologies used by LeisureLoop. Our current website code does not include configured analytics or advertising trackers. We use limited browser storage for essential operation and preferences.

1. What are cookies and similar technologies?

Cookies are small files stored by a website in your browser. Similar technologies include local storage and session storage, which can retain information in your browser without being cookies.

2. What LeisureLoop currently stores

  • Authentication session: the Supabase Auth client is configured to persist sign-in sessions in browser storage so that you remain signed in and the session can be refreshed. Treat these session tokens as confidential; do not share your device or credentials.
  • Chosen location (ll_location): remembers a city you selected or a city returned after you expressly choose to share your device location. It is used to personalise the location shown on the site.
  • Role preview (ll_role): remembers a role selection in the current preview flow.
  • Notice dismissal (ll_cookie_notice_dismissed): remembers that you dismissed the informational storage notice.

These items are stored in browser local storage, not necessarily as cookies. The custom-auth API code can issue a strictly necessary HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax ll_session cookie for a 14-day session, but the main login page currently uses Supabase Auth and persists its session in browser storage. Confirm which authentication flow is actually active and inspect production response headers before launch.

3. Analytics and advertising

We did not find Google Analytics, Google Tag Manager, Meta Pixel, advertising tags or another analytics script in the homepage and sign-in code reviewed for this update. We therefore do not currently describe any analytics or advertising cookies as active. If analytics, advertising or other non-essential tracking is introduced, LeisureLoop should update this policy and block those technologies until the visitor has made a valid, informed opt-in choice where required by applicable law.

4. Third-party resources

The current pages load some resources from third parties, including Google Fonts, an Unsplash-hosted hero photograph, Supabase JavaScript/authentication resources delivered through public CDNs, and OpenStreetMap's Nominatim reverse-geocoding service when you actively request device location. The codebase also contains a separate custom-auth API that can issue an HttpOnly ll_session cookie, although the main login form currently uses Supabase Auth; confirm whether that separate flow is enabled before launch. These are resource or service requests, not embedded video players or social widgets. The relevant provider may receive technical request information such as your IP address, browser details and request metadata. The Nominatim request is made only after you activate the location button.

5. Managing browser storage

You can clear site data or block storage through your browser settings. Clearing authentication storage will sign you out; clearing the chosen-location value or notice preference will reset those preferences. Blocking essential storage may prevent login or parts of the service from working.

6. Cookie consent

India's DPDP Act does not create a blanket cookie-specific consent rule. The legal basis depends on the personal data involved, the purpose and the applicable provisions. Our present approach is to limit storage to essential authentication and user-selected preferences and to avoid analytics/advertising trackers. This does not remove the need for a valid privacy notice and lawful basis for any personal-data processing. If the site later serves users in jurisdictions with stricter cookie rules, separate opt-in consent may be required before non-essential cookies or trackers are set.

7. Changes

We will update this policy if the technologies used by LeisureLoop change. The date at the top shows the latest revision.

Technical verification still required: This policy reflects a source-code review, not a live browser/network audit. Before launch, inspect production cookies, local/session storage, response headers and all third-party requests on desktop and mobile. Remove any provider or tracker that is not necessary, documented and approved.
